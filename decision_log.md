| 2026-10-26 | Validation labels are judged mainly from single Sentinel-2 dates (20 Sep–4 Dec 2025, 10 m, true and false colour), fetched by a small extra openEO job, with the 5-day composite strips kept for context. The 240 points stay as drawn on 26 Oct. | Label from the composite strips (the plan of 25 Oct); redraw the composite strips larger with a hollow marker; look up every point in Copernicus Browser | On the composite strips from the district run, the cross covered the block being judged, and burnt and bare fields showed in much the same mauve. In the pilot box, more than half of the composite burnt area never met the rule with NIR ≤ 0.10 on any single date from 15 Oct to 24 Nov when it was clear (17 dates checked): most mapped burns are only moderately dark, and in a 20 m five-day median they look much like bare soil. Looking up 240 points one by one would take hours more. Cost: one more openEO job (the district composites cost 8 credits) and the `netCDF4` package to read its files. |
| 2026-10-26 | The burn rule gains a third condition: NIR (B08) reflectance ≤ 0.13 in the burn window, alongside NBR ≤ −0.05 and MIRBI ≥ 1.65. Calibration at Checkpoint 3 tunes all three thresholds. | Keep the two-index rule until the labels are in; stricter NBR and MIRBI thresholds | In the 10 km pilot (run 26 Oct 2026), the two-index rule marked 91% of harvested land burnt (6,737 of 7,374 ha). In the pilot's single-date images, bare soil after harvest falls in the same NBR and MIRBI range as fresh burns, so stricter thresholds would cut both. NIR separates them better: fresh burns are mostly below about 0.13, bare soil mostly about 0.15 and above. With the condition, 25% is marked burnt (1,872 ha), and 48 of the 50 VIIRS detections inside the box still have a mapped burn within 375 m in the matching time span. Waiting for the labels would have drawn the validation sample from a map that called nearly everything burnt. The 48-of-50 check is weak, though: in the peak windows, land near detections is mapped burnt about as often as land far from them. The labels will measure accuracy. |

| 2026-10-25 | Notebook 02 builds 5-day median Sentinel-2 composites at 20 m on openEO (red, NIR, SWIR 1 and 2), resampled by nearest neighbour and stored as 16-bit integers. A pixel counts as harvested when NDVI falls below 0.35 after reaching 0.6, and as burnt when NBR ≤ −0.05 and MIRBI ≥ 1.65 in a harvested window. The thresholds are provisional until Checkpoint 3. | Single-date change detection between consecutive clear dates (the earlier plan); 10-day composites; an ESA WorldCover cropland mask | Five-day medians fill most cloud gaps while still dating harvest and burning to a 5-day window. Nearest neighbour keeps the scene classes intact. The 16-bit output halves the download, using a workaround openEO's developers describe ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/Open-EO/openeo-geotrellis-extensions/issues/873)). |
| 2026-10-25 | The cloud mask keeps scene classes 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7 (dark area, vegetation, bare soil, water, unclassified), and a pilot job checks which classes burnt-looking pixels fall in. | Keep only vegetation and bare soil; a dilated cloud mask | Fresh burn scars are dark, so the scene classification may put them in the dark-area or water class. | 2026-10-25 | Validation: 240 points drawn at random from the map's classes (100 burnt, 100 harvested but not burnt, 40 other), each at the centre of a 3 × 3 block of one class. They are labelled blind from image strips of all 18 windows. Half the points choose the thresholds and the other half check them, with area-weighted accuracy ([Stehman 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/01431161.2014.930207)) and a burnt area estimated from the labels with a 95% confidence interval ([Olofsson et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2014.02.015)). | About 300 points labelled in QGIS (the earlier plan); a simple random sample | Burnt area is a minority of the district, so a simple random sample would put few points on burns. Points from every class let accuracy be weighted by area. Image strips generated by the notebook are quicker to label than QGIS, which fits the 1 Nov checkpoint. Points avoid field edges, so edge accuracy isn't measured. |
